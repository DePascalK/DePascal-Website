What you'll do

You can choose among the following topics:

1) Topic 1: Space weather missions and instruments design

ESA is developing an enhanced Space Weather Sensor System to provide data for forecasting and to monitor Space Weather effects. Due to the to the asymmetry and complexity of Earth's magnetosphere, the involved particle environment and its dynamics, it is necessary to capture the state of the magnetic field and the particle distribution in a sufficiently large number of sampling points around the Earth, such that it allows state-monitoring and modelling of the involved processes with sufficient accuracy and timeliness. An important aspect for the realisation of observation systems for Space Safety is the need of high reliability, sufficiently long lifetime and low data latencies as the data will be used in operational purposes.

For this, ESA is currently implementing and studying multiple dedicated Space Weather missions, from hosted payloads to nanosatellites and small satellites, that will be flying in a wide range of orbits. This will allow you to look into the different steps of an ESA space mission, from early study to mission implementation and management of flying missions.

Your role would be to contribute to the mission development of upcoming missions, through analysis of the mission performance utilising different Space Weather models, instrument simulations and data, and mission analysis tools. These analyses will provide insight into the optimisation of the mission design and operations concept and will support the Space Weather team in guiding ongoing industry developments.

2) Topic 2: Space Weather Product Front-End and UI/UX design

ESA coordinates a large-scale project called the ESA Space Weather Service Network. One of the main roles of the Network is to provide a user interface through the ESA Space Weather Service portal (swe.ssa.esa.int) where dozens of European experts display hundreds of different space weather products, from solar observations, to numerical space weather forecasts, all the way down to the tracing of space weather effects on electric grid infrastructure. Academia and industry provide very rich, complete, and high-quality data which is made available through APIs and machine-machine interfaces. The goal of this internship is to investigate upgrading of the front-end layer of this complex system providing a modern, ergonomic, and visually attractive interface with enhanced user experience.

Your role would be to design a prototype of a modern front-end interface for some of the most recent space weather products available for display in the ESA Space Weather portal. Your knowledge of UX/UI and human-machine interfaces will be put to the test, as well as your ability to propose high-quality professional graphic designs. Your tasks will include a detailed report on existing UX and graphic design guidelines from the Agency, and a guideline on key concepts for excellent web design (as expressed for example in the book “Don’t make me think” by Steve Krug). You will be asked to propose a visual identity document, a usability analysis document, and mock-ups of front-end designs for the new space weather products. You will finalise your internship by developing a first software prototype of the new front-end and its interface with the back-end.

The focus of this project will be to define a professional user interface and visual identity. The Agency encourages candidates with high interest and expertise in user experience (UX), user interface (UI) and human-machine interface (HMI) design to demonstrate their ability to transform high-quality data into high-value information for our end-users. A tailored CV and motivation letter highlighting how your profile fits this topic is the best way to apply for this internship.

About the team

The ESA Space Safety Department takes care of all potential hazards and threats from space or in space, which can impact human activities, infrastructure, or related applications. The spectrum of tasks ranges from gathering the basic understanding of space hazards originating from our Sun, from human space debris, or from asteroids potentially colliding with the Earth. It also addresses basic approaches to mitigate these hazards and to implement space systems end-to-end in a sustainable and eco-compatible way. Moreover, several corner stone missions are underway to demonstrate basic methods of space weather forecasting, asteroid deflection and active space debris removal from orbit.

In execution of these tasks, the Space Safety Department has the following entities:

  • Mission Implementation Division (OPS-SM);
  • COSMIC Project Manageent Office (OPS-SC);
  • Space Weather Office (OPS-SW);
  • Planetary Defence Office (OPS-SP);
  • Space Debris Office (OPS-SD);
  • Clean Space & Circular Economy Office (OPS-SY);
  • Frequency Management Office (OPS-SF);
  • Programme Coordination Office (OPS-SX).

Space Weather Office is responsible for developing the ESA Space Weather System that allow nowcasting and forecasting space weather, i.e., the enormous eruptions in the Sun that impact the environment everywhere in our solar system. When these eruptions hit the Earth, they can cause major damage in the infrastructure on the surface, damage satellites and endanger the health of the astronauts in space. Geomagnetic storms triggered by the solar events can cause blackouts in power grids, disturb satellite navigation and communication, disturb aviation, transport, and many other functions that we are used to in our daily life. Energetic particles from the solar events can also damage space probes that we have sent to other planets in the solar system. This is why Space Weather Office is developing space weather forecasting capability for other planets like Mercury, Venus, Mars and Jupiter. Space Weather Office is also responsible for development and implementation of the satellite missions for space weather monitoring to make the space weather services to the users possible.
