What you'll do

Trajectory optimisation plays a central role in mission design and analysis at ESA’s European Space Operations Centre (ESOC). Interplanetary trajectories, lunar tours, and other complex missions all rely on sophisticated global search and optimisation methods. Typically, coarse initial solutions are refined through local, gradient-based techniques to achieve the required mission accuracy and feasibility.

The Mission Analysis Section at ESOC employs different tools for trajectory design and optimisation, and has defined a unified software framework called SALTO. This framework implements an innovative approach for interplanetary trajectory design, separating the energy-based unphased solutions from the phasing problem, although still requiring a certain degree of expert input in order to efficiently obtain mission solutions.

You will contribute to the development of SALTO, with a particular focus on global optimisation techniques, to find wide ranges of mission possibilities once an interplanetary mission profile is established. Your tasks include, but are not limited to:

  • Investigating the integration of global optimisation methods (e.g. genetic algorithms, differential evolution, or other metaheuristics) within the SALTO framework;
  • improving the usability, robustness, and automation of the tool based on user experience and mission requirements;
  • enhancing the performance of SALTO through parallelisation and algorithmic optimisation;
  • investigating the use of General Purpose Graphical Processing Units (GP-GPUs) to accelerate the global search methods.

The use of GPUs is particularly of interest for this internship. ESOC has developed a set of GPU-based tools, which can be used to accelerate the global optimisation methods. The position offers the opportunity to contribute to the future of ESA’s mission design capabilities, combining theoretical research with hands-on software development and validation on real-world problems.

About the team

You will be part of the Mission Analysis Section, Flight Dynamics Division, Ground Systems Engineering Department.

The Mission Analysis Section is entrusted with mission studies for future terrestrial, lunar and interplanetary missions as well as scientific missions located in planetary or lunar libration points. The emphasis is typically on trajectory and attitude related aspects, and on supporting ground segment design and operations. This includes trade-offs for selection of the nominal mission trajectory, definition of manoeuvering strategies, optimisation of orbital manoeuvres (low- and high-thrust propulsion, including rocket ascent trajectories), calculation of propellant budget, analysis of launch window, analysis of orbit perturbations and navigation. The task also includes the development of the necessary analytical and numerical methods and software tools.
