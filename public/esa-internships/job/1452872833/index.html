What you'll do

The internship will exploit accelerometer measurements acquired during the Venus Express aerobraking campaign to constrain the representation of atmospheric variability and gravity-wave activity used in EnVision aerobraking simulations. Following the reconstruction of atmospheric-density profiles from the VEX measurements, Venus Climate Database simulations will be generated for conditions representative of the corresponding VEX passes. The simulations will be compared with the observed density levels, variability and spectral content.

You will develop a parametric representation of the measured density perturbations—for example, based on a superposition of wave trains—and derive distributions for the associated amplitudes, wavelengths and phases. The ability of both the Venus Climate Database and the parametric model to reproduce the flight observations and their resulting aerodynamic effects will be assessed.

The resulting modelling approach will then be applied to representative EnVision trajectories to generate reference atmospheric-density disturbance scenarios. These scenarios will support, in particular, Attitude and Orbit Control System simulations and thermo-mechanical fatigue analyses of spacecraft appendages, including the solar arrays. Time permitting, you will also contribute to relevant EnVision aerobraking Monte Carlo analyses.

Throughout the internship, you will collaborate closely with the mission analysis, system engineering, payload and science teams, as well as environment experts of the technical directorate. The internship offers a unique opportunity to gain end-to-end insight into planetary mission design and to make a meaningful contribution to the preparation of a major European planetary exploration mission.

About the team

EnVision was selected by ESA’s Science Programme Committee as the fifth Medium-class mission in the Agency’s Cosmic Vision plan, with a launch in the early 2030s from ESA’s Spaceport in Kourou. EnVision will be the first mission to investigate Venus from its inner core to its upper atmosphere, characterising the interaction between its different envelopes: its atmosphere, surface/subsurface and interior. It aims to provide a holistic view of Venus, studying the planet’s history, activity, and climate.

EnVision’s science payload consists of VeraSAR, a dual polarisation S-band radar also operating as microwave radiometer and altimeter, which will map the surface of Venus; three optical spectrometers (VenSpec-M, VenSpec-U and VenSpec-H) designed to observe the surface and atmosphere of Venus; and the Subsurface Radar Sounder (VeSper), a High Frequency (HF) sounding radar to probe the top kilometres of subsurface. These are complemented by a Radio Science investigation exploiting the spacecraft Telemetry Tracking and Command (TT&C) system to map the planet’s gravity field and constrain its internal structure, and to measure the composition and structure of Venus’ atmosphere.

The EnVision project team is responsible for implementing the EnVision mission, composed of all its elements (Spacecraft, Instruments, Ground Segment).

The role of the Mission and Performance team within the EnVision project is to ensure that the mission will fulfil its mission requirements by rigorous requirements apportioning, defining and controlling interfaces between elements, and verifying the requirements by establishing end-to-end performance budgets, supported as needed by mission performance simulations. The team is directly interfacing with all stakeholders of the mission (industry, the mission operations centre at ESOC in Germany, the Science Operations centre at ESAC in Spain, the launcher provider, the science team and the instrument teams).

The mission and performance team develops and maintains mission-level tools and budgets (e.g. Delta V budget, aerobraking scenarios), including mission performance simulators for both the aerobraking and scientific parts of the mission, perform relevant mission-level analyses and lead mission-level trade-offs.
