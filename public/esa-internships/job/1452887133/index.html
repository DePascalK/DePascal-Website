What you'll do

Under the supervision of a tutor in the navigation system definition section, you will shape the future Solar System Internet (SSI) Navigation system. You will develop simulators and analysis tools to characterise the performance of SSI at both system and user level. In addition, you will support the definition of an SSI In-Orbit Demonstrator (IOD) mission in cislunar space, aimed at de-risking key SSI technologies, and enabling future lunar science and exploration activities.

In particular, you will:

  • develop end-to-end simulation tools for System Internet Navigation Performance assessment;
  • perform mission analysis for a Solar System In-Orbit Demonstrator in cislunar space;
  • analyse and address navigation use cases for future Solar System missions;
  • investigate autonomous positioning, navigation and time synchronisation concepts for deep-space users and infrastructure nodes;
  • contribute to the definition and evaluation of SSI architectures and deployment scenarios.

What you need

You should demonstrate experience in either one of the following fields:

  • software development in MATLAB or Python;
  • orbital mechanics, astrodynamics and space mission analysis;
  • telecommunications.

About the team

The Navigation Systems Definition Section (TEC-SEN) is leading, or providing support to, the Agency’s activities that require expertise in the field of Positioning, Navigation, Timing (PNT) and Geolocation Systems definition, design and development. The PNT and Geolocation domain covers the End-to-End (E2E) Engineering of Positioning, Navigation, Timing and Geolocation Systems, at System, Space, Ground and User segments level, covering links, elements and equipment, system concepts, and all related techniques and technologies, for Earth, Space and Solar System applications, System/Segment/Payload System architectures and performance, signal-in-space and signal processing, GNSS receivers, algorithms, OD&TS, formation-flying and RF/Optical metrology.

Candidates interested are encouraged to visit the ESA website: http://www.esa.int
