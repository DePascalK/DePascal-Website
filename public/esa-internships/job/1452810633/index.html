What you'll do

Space communication is often characterised by long round-trip delays and less reliable communication links. Communication links are typically planned in advance and are only available for limited time. Data rates on the individual links may vary between a few kilobits per second and several gigabits per second and a continuous end-to-end path may not be available. To overcome these challenges, Delay or Disruption Tolerant Networking (DTN) communication architectures have been proposed and related communication protocols have been standardised for the space domain (Bundle Protocol, Licklider Transmission Protocol). These protocols are being implemented by ESA, simulated, and validated for various scenarios, including Earth Observation and lunar or Mars exploration scenarios.

As an intern, you will contribute to the following tasks:

  • test and validation of protocol implementations and prototypes;
  • deployment and validation of prototype operational systems on ground to prepare DTN communication experiments and demonstrations;
  • support the execution of DTN experiments and demonstrations with external partners (space agencies, commercial entities, academia);
  • depending on your interests, you could also be involved in areas currently under active development and standardisation, such as Bundle Protocol (BP) naming schemes, hybrid BP-IP networks or mission operations for DTN-enabled spacecraft.

Through this internship, you will gain substantial experience in ground segment software development at ESA, obtain a comprehensive understanding of space communication systems, and engage with advanced technologies for future networked space communications aimed at establishing the Solar System Internet.

What you need

Experience with software development in Java, Rust or C++ on Linux is required. Knowledge and background in terrestrial networking (IP, TCP, UDP) or space communication protocols is an asset.

About the team

Our team develops ground-segment software for space communication systems and for the European Space Agency’s worldwide network of ground stations. We investigate, develop and validate new communication technologies that support reliable data exchange with spacecraft and between distributed space and ground systems.

Our activities combine software engineering, communication protocol development, system integration, simulation and operational experimentation. We also contribute to international standardisation and collaborate with other space agencies, European industry, universities and research organisations.

As part of the team, you will gain experience in an international engineering environment and contribute to technologies supporting future missions to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars and beyond. Your activities will form part of wider efforts to establish interoperable communication services for a future Solar System Internet.
