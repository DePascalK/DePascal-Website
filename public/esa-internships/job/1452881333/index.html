What you'll do

You can choose among the following topics:

1) Topic 1: AI for Advanced Digital Control and Failure Prediction of DC/DC Converters

This topic will be hosted in the Power Systems Architecture section in the TEC-EP Division.
The objective is to investigate how Artificial Intelligence (AI) can enhance the performance, robustness, and autonomy of digitally controlled DC/DC converters for space applications. The work may include AI-assisted control strategies, adaptive tuning of control parameters, anomaly detection, health monitoring, and predictive failure analysis based on telemetry and operational data. You will explore techniques such as machine learning, neural networks, and digital twins to improve converter efficiency, stability, reliability, and fault tolerance while supporting future intelligent power management architectures.

2) Topic 2: AI assisted Design of DC/DC converters

This topic will be hosted in the Power Systems Architecture section in the TEC-EP Division.
The objective is to explore the application of Artificial Intelligence to support and accelerate the design of DC/DC converters. Areas of investigation may include topology selection, semiconductor sizing and loss estimation, magnetic component design, control system development, and multi-objective optimisation. The work will assess how specialised AI agents, combined with engineering design tools, can automate design tasks, improve design quality, reduce development time, and support engineers throughout the converter design process. The study will focus on creating efficient AI-driven workflows for power electronics engineering.

What you need

Knowledge and background in DC/DC power conversion, power converter control and FPGA programming is an asset.

About the team

The Power Systems Architecture Section supports space missions and technology research and development activities by defining and optimising spacecraft power system architectures. The team contributes to the sizing and design of power generation, power conditioning and power distribution systems that safely regulate and deliver electrical power to spacecraft equipment.
As an intern, you will work in a multidisciplinary engineering environment, collaborating with specialists in power electronics and spacecraft systems. You will use advanced engineering tools and simulation techniques to assess electrical performance, analyse system dynamics and investigate failure modes under different space mission conditions. This provides a unique opportunity to contribute to innovative technologies that support future space missions.
